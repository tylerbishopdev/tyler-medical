Why this 2017 study is worth re-reading today:
  • The mass was called out as "most likely related to the dermis rather than gynecomastia" and as having "no appreciable breast tissue." This was a dermal/subdermal lesion, not glandular breast tissue — despite being worked up and later excised under a gynecomastia framing.
  • By patient report the lump was first noticed around 2012 ("felt it 5 years ago"), resolved, and recurred — i.e. a hard subcutaneous lesion at a fixed anatomic site that came and went, then persisted. That behavior matches the pattern described elsewhere in this record: lesions that begin as hard subcutaneous lumps and evolve at recurring sites.
  • This predates the 2021 onset of the current progressive skin disease by roughly nine years.
  • Limit: BI-RADS 2 means benign by imaging. The mass was excised in August 2017; the surgical pathology from that excision is not present in these records. Retrieving that pathology report — and, if blocks were retained, requesting mucin and amyloid stains on the archived tissue — would be a low-cost, potentially high-yield step.
